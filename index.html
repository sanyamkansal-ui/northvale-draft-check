Northvale Draft Check

An AI model drafted a customer notice about a utility rate application. Before anyone could publish it, every sentence was checked against the approved facts from the filing. A separate set of 20 test claims shows how reliable the check itself is.

How the check runs

  1. Approved facts from the filing
  2. Written acceptance rules
  3. AI drafts the notice
  4. Each sentence checked against the facts
  5. Fix or remove what fails
  6. A person signs off

      Testing the check itself

      A check is only useful if it catches the mistakes that matter without flagging good sentences. Twenty test claims were written with their expected labels first: ten faithful, two with the wrong certainty and eight seeded errors of the kinds that reach customers (a percentage on the wrong base, outage hours confused with outage counts, survey respondents turned into "customers", a proposal described as approved). ChatGPT then labelled all twenty blind, using only the facts and rules above.

      Failure log

      Case 2 "Your bill will go up by $4.12 a month starting May 1, 2027." The key said REWORD, for stating a proposal as certain. The checker said CONTRADICTED, for that and for applying a typical-customer figure to every reader's bill. On review the checker is right: "your bill" applies the 750 kWh customer's figure to every reader, which breaks R2 as well as R3, and the key missed it. The key is corrected. Either label keeps the sentence out of the notice.

      Running this with a customer

      Activation

      • Agree the first use case and its success criteria before setup: no contradicted claim reaches a customer, and a reviewer signs off a notice in under 15 minutes.
      • Build the approved-facts pack with the regulatory team from the filing itself, one numbered fact per line.
      • Run the first drafts side by side with the team's usual process and compare.

      Training users

      • Teach reviewers to read the claim table first and the prose second.
      • Show the four labels with this page's examples, especially the base and certainty mistakes.
      • Keep the rule list short and in plain language, so a new analyst can apply it without the tool.

      Measuring adoption and value

      • Share of drafts released after one fix cycle.
      • Reviewer minutes per notice, before and after.
      • Catch rate and false-flag rate on a test set refreshed each quarter.

      Feedback to the product team

      • Log every miss and every false flag with the sentence, the fact and the rule involved.
      • Group repeat patterns (base errors, certainty errors) into prompt or rule changes.
      • Tell the customer when a change affects how their drafts are checked.
